**Restated pause trigger (c) will not fire on the issuer that actually changes.** Trigger (c) keys
on *the accounting cost-of-debt channel refusing*. MPWR's guard **does not fire** (no filed debt →

Two things follow, and both are the orchestrator's call, not the builder's:

1. **MPWR joins the pre-registered affected set**, with a stated expected direction (down) and
   magnitude (FCFF margin −77…−224 bps, i.e. `2 × interest × (1 − tax)` per year).
2. **Trigger (c) needs a second condition** keyed on a published FCFF-margin shift for any issuer

**§6.2's "direction, magnitude and affected-issuer set" claim is now writable**, and W2b may not
describe COF/DAL/CHTR/BKR as the issuers it fixes: it fixes MPWR and leaves those four unchanged.

**One risk the measurement raises and cannot close.** MPWR was found only because the cohort
happened to contain it; it was named in no plan revision, no directive and no review round. The
cohort is 29 names. The archetype — an operating issuer with net cash, no debt, and a positive

`{MPWR}` within this cohort and is unmeasured outside it.** A wider scan is cheap (25 s over 29

## What surprised me

1. **MPWR.** Twelve straight years of positive `InterestIncomeExpenseNonoperatingNet` on an
   operating, gate-cohort issuer, and it appears in no plan revision, no directive, and no review
   round. Four revisions argued about the sign convention using COF, DAL, CHTR, BKR, LIN and BAC —
   and the single issuer whose published number the change actually moves was in the gate cohort
   the whole time. The pre-registration was arguing about the wrong names.

2. **Both traces were half right, and the half each got right made the other look wrong.** COF
   files positive (trace A). DAL/CHTR/BKR file negative and are bit-identical (trace B). Each side
   generalized its correct case to the other's, and neither generalization holds.

3. **`InterestIncomeExpenseNet` is not a bank-only concept, and it is not sign-stable across
   issuers.** COF and BAC file it **positive** in the tens of billions; CHTR, DAL and BKR file it
   **negative** in the hundreds to thousands of millions. Same tag, opposite sign, both correct
   under the XBRL credit-balance convention. A rule keyed on the *value's* sign rather than on the
   *concept* would have been a per-issuer branch in disguise; the declarative per-qname convention
   is genuinely the right shape.

4. **The concept rotates mid-history, silently.** CHTR runs `InterestExpense` 2010–2013, then
   `InterestIncomeExpenseNet` 2014–2023, then `InterestIncomeExpenseNonoperatingNet` 2024–2025 —
   three concepts, two measurement bases, one series, and today the `.abs()` makes all three look
   identical. DAL is worse: it flips to `InterestIncomeExpenseNet` for 2011 only, back to
   `InterestExpense` for 2012–2017, then to the net concepts from 2018. **A basis change inside one
   issuer's own series is invisible in every artefact this repo currently produces.**

5. **`.abs()` and the guard are not the same lever.** The guard fires only where a year also has
   filed debt. MPWR has none, so its guard never fires while its published FCFF moves by ~2% of
   revenue. T2.7's ruling and LD-1's removal have **disjoint** blast radii, and the plan's pause
   triggers are keyed to only one of them.

6. **EPAM files no interest concept at all** — sixteen years, zero facts in the entire equivalence
   class. Reported as absent, never as zero. Unrelated to the sign question, but a coverage hole
   nobody has named.

7. **The reconstruction matched production on every issuer-year.** Zero mismatches between
   `|filed|` (rebuilt from raw companyfacts through the declared merge order) and the live
   `FcfPoint::interest_expense_dollars`. That is what makes the "winning qname" column trustworthy
   rather than plausible.
